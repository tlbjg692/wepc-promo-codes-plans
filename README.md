## Does the annual code actually save you money?

Take Korea SEJONG, the one plan where all three billing cycles were published: **AUD 22.41 monthly, AUD 65.43 quarterly, AUD 239.04 annually**. Divide the annual total by twelve and you get **AUD 19.92/month**, about 11% below the monthly rate.

That's less than the 20% in the code name. Cycle pricing gets set before the coupon, so the percentage describes the coupon's face value rather than the gap between annual and monthly. Look at the cart total, not the code name. If the annual price already carries a discount, the 20% won't land the way you expect.

On cheap plans the saving is small in absolute terms. A 20% cut on a AUD 4.24 line is about 85 cents a month, roughly AUD 10 across a year. You'd be committing around AUD 41 up front to save ten dollars, on a box whose refund window is three days or 10GB of traffic. At that price, pay monthly until the IP proves itself.

Where the annual code earns its keep is on the expensive plans and only where the region qualifies. AUD 239.04 for a year of Korean dual-ISP residential is a real saving against 12 × 22.41. AUD 40 for a year of Australian shared bandwidth isn't worth the lock-in.

## Pick by workload

**Posting short video, one or two accounts.** Entry tier in the target region. AU_1120 at 4.24, US LA Basic at 5.23, UK Basic or German DE Basic at around 8.90. A 1TB allowance is far more than a few daily uploads consume.

**Live streaming.** Bandwidth and routing decide this, not core count. Japan TikTok Dedicated (200Mbps, 2TB, three-network direct, no transit) and South Korea TikTok Live Dedicated (100Mbps, 2TB, no transit) both list at AUD 18.90. Korea SEJONG at 22.41 adds a residential dual-ISP address with China Telecom and China Mobile described as direct and China Unicom routed.

**Account matrix.** The two US dual-ISP home plans sit on separate 216 and 38 segments at AUD 12.90 each. Spreading accounts across different ranges is the reason to pick one over the other. Confirm the actual range at checkout, since segment stock moves.

**You need transit, not a server.** Hong Kong Relay: AUD 12.90, 500GB on a 200Mbps port, three-network direct out of Hong Kong. It's built to relay traffic, not to host anything.

**Skip Vietnam if TikTok is the target.** A March review of the Vietnam plan found the native IP failed to unlock TikTok and Gemini, though ChatGPT, Claude and Netflix worked. CPU benchmarks came out weak, and China Telecom and Unicom routes detour enough that the provider itself recommends transit. Traffic is metered in both directions, which eats a 2TB allowance faster than the number suggests, and SOCKS, HTTP and TLS protocols are banned on that plan.

## Limits worth knowing before you pay

**The refund is 3 days or 10GB, whichever comes first.** Not three days and 10GB. If you plan to stress-test with video uploads, 10GB disappears quickly, and a third-party review makes exactly this point about the data cap.

**Don't run Shadowsocks on non-relay packages.** That's the provider's own instruction, published alongside its setup tutorials.

**Several regions need transit to work well from mainland China.** Australia needs it for Telecom users, Taiwan for live streaming, Singapore for Telecom, plus Thailand, the UK for Telecom and Mobile, Germany for Telecom, and Malaysia for Telecom. Japan, Korea and the US NY 4837 route are listed as no-transit-needed.

**Stock is erratic.** UK and German entry plans sell out, and popular regions drop to zero availability during peak demand. If a plan you want shows out of stock, it isn't gone permanently.

**Currency and payment.** Everything is priced in AUD. Select AUD when you register or PayPal won't appear as an option. Alipay and credit cards work either way.

**Support runs on local hours.** Live chat covers 9am to 9pm in the provider's timezone, with tickets for everything else. A review notes that complex technical tickets move slowly.

**New Zealand routes through the United States** for all three Chinese carriers, so latency will be worse than Japan, Korea or the US even though the IP is genuinely New Zealand.

## FAQ

**Which code should I try first?**
`WePCMON90` for a monthly or quarterly order. `WePCYEAR80` if you're paying annually and your region isn't Taiwan, Singapore or the US residential series. `WePC9929MON85` or `WePC9929YEAR70` only if you're buying a US Los Angeles CN2 GIA or 9929 plan.

**Can I combine the monthly and annual codes?**
No. One code per order, and the code has to match the billing cycle you selected.

**Does the annual code work on Taiwan or Singapore?**
No. Both are named exclusions, along with the US residential IP series. Other region or model exclusions may apply.

**My code was rejected. What now?**
Check the region and model first, then confirm you're on wepc.au and not a similarly named site. One code per order. If it still fails, live chat during business hours or a ticket will get you an answer faster than retrying variations.

**How does the refund work in practice?**
Three days from delivery or 10GB of traffic, whichever you hit first. Test the IP against the platform you actually care about inside that window.
